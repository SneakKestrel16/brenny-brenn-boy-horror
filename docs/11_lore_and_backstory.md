## 1. Rules for the lore

The lore sits under doc 01 and never changes it.

- **No new rules.** Lore explains why a rule feels the way it does. It never adds a condition, a tell or an exception.
- **Never explain outright.** Players piece it together from notes, clippings and the farm itself. Nothing in the game states the whole story.
- **Diegetic and text only.** Notes, signs and the Dawn Report. No cutscenes, no narration, no captions and no voice (D-019 and the caption ruling stand).
- **Glimpsed, never seen.** No lore image shows the creature or the boy clearly. No photo of Brennen exists anywhere in the game.
- **Never say the day is safe.** No note or clipping implies it.
- **The scarecrow stays unexplained.** Doc 01 says the moving scarecrow is "never explained". Section 4 gives the team's private reason; nothing in game states it.
- **The flicker stays the one flicker.** No lore effect flickers a light.

## 2. The story

### 2.1 The short version (for the team)

The farm belonged to the Brenns: Jeremy, Jill and their three children, Julia, Brennen and Brynlee. Brennen, called Brenny, hardly spoke in words; he spoke in other people's voices. On the Harvest Moon of 1958 he was caught in one of his father's bear traps in the corn. He called for help in his mother's voice, his father's, his sisters' and the neighbours'. Julia started for the corn and Jeremy stopped her. Brynlee said one of the calls was really him, and nobody listened to a six-year-old. The family decided he was "doing voices" again, left him to come in when he was hungry, and pushed the festival cart to town to pay the bank. In the morning the trap was shut and empty and the corn had grown thick around it. Brenny never came in. The bank took the farm anyway. It has been leased out ever since, a season at a time, and every tenant hears a boy in the corn.

### 2.2 The long version

**The Brenns.** Jeremy and Jill Brenn and their three children farmed the clearing at the end of the town road through the 1950s. They owed Farmers & Merchants Bank of Halvers Creek more than the farm made, and every year came down to the Harvest Moon festival, the Prize Pumpkin and a payment at dawn.

**The sisters.** Julia, fourteen, was the eldest. She ran the family's table at the town stand, counted every coin twice, and was the one Jeremy trusted with the farm. Brynlee, six, was the youngest. She followed Brenny everywhere and was the only one in the family who could always tell his voices from the real thing.

**The boy.** Their only son, Brennen, was nine. He rarely used his own voice. He copied: the dinner call, the pump handle, the hogs, the church bell, his father's hoe in the dirt, the neighbours arguing over the fence. He could throw a voice into the corn so well that Jill would answer her own call. The town found it funny and then it found it wrong. The kids at the stand made up a rhyme, and the nickname stuck: the Brenny Brenn boy.

Jeremy hated the voices. "Use your own," he'd say. "If you want something, use your own voice." Brenny mostly didn't.

**The traps.** Something had been at the crops that autumn, hogs or thieves. Jeremy set bear traps in the corn and painted each trap's outline on the pegboard in the shed so he'd know which were out. His rule was simple: if it's not on the board, it's in the corn.

**The Harvest Moon, 1958.** The payment was due at dawn. The family loaded the Prize Pumpkin onto the festival cart in the barn. Then the generator quit and the barn went dark.

Brenny was out in the corn. He'd gone to fetch something, or to hide, or to play. Nobody agreed later. He stepped in a trap that was not on the board.

He called for help. First in Jill's voice, then in Jeremy's, then Julia's and Brynlee's, then the neighbours'. Every call came from somewhere that person couldn't be. Jeremy stood at the barn door and listened. "He's doing voices," he said. "Don't answer him. He'll come in when he's hungry."

Julia took a lantern and started for the corn. Jeremy caught her arm at the door and sent her back to the cart. Brynlee tugged at her mother's sleeve. "That one's really him," she said. Nobody listened to a six-year-old.

They pushed the cart to the gate. In town, the church bell rang three times for the start of the festival. The pumpkin judged Medium. It wasn't enough.

**The morning.** At dawn the trap was shut and empty. The corn around it had grown a ring a man's height overnight, wild and thick, and it would not cut. Jeremy went in after him every day for a week. He came out with nothing but the trap, which he hung back on its outline. Julia went in with him and never forgave him for the night before. Brynlee sat on the porch each dusk and whistled two short, the way their father called Brenny in for supper.

On the eighth day the bank foreclosed. The Brenns left. The search was called off at harvest.

**Since then.** The bank leases the farm one season at a time to anyone who'll sign. The terms never change: seven days, a payment due, the Prize Pumpkin, the festival cart. The town rings the bell three times every dusk now, and the older folks say it's so the Brenn boy knows to come in. Nobody has farmed the place long enough to pay it off.

The thing in the corn still copies voices. It still sets Jeremy's traps. It still comes when someone's caught and nobody comes back for them. Whether it's Brenny, what's left of him, or something that learned his trick, the lore never says.

## 3. The four bodies

Doc 01: one body per season, all hunt the same. The lore reason: the thing in the corn wears what the farm left out there that night. Each body is a different "costume", never a different creature.

| Body | What it wore that night | Lore hook |
|---|---|---|
| Gaunt thing | Nothing. What's left of a boy who never came in for supper. | The hungry one: "He'll come in when he's hungry." |
| Scarecrow | Jeremy's own coat and a feed sack, off the field scarecrow by the trap. | Ember eyes for the barn lantern he watched through the stalks. |
| Boar brute | The Brenns' prize hog, which broke its chain and ran into the corn that same night. Iron collar, chain still dragging. | The "hogs at the crops" Jeremy blamed. |
| Corn husk | The corn itself, grown around him overnight, with something still warm at the centre. | The glowing heart sits where the trap closed. |

## 4. Why the rules are the rules

Team-facing. These are reasons behind existing doc 01 rules, for writers, artists and audio. None of them changes a rule.

| Doc 01 rule | The lore reason |
|---|---|
| **Voice mimicry** | It's the only way Brenny ever talked. |
| **Fakes come from a place the teammate can't be** | That was the tell on the Harvest Moon, and Jeremy ignored it. |
| **It favours the dead's voices** | Nobody came back for him either. The dead are the ones who'll understand. |
| **The whistle is unfakeable** | Jeremy called him in for supper with two short whistles. It's the one sound Brenny never learned to make. |
| **The ghost flicker is unfakeable** | The dead are on the farmers' side. The boy never touches the lights. |
| **Lit buildings are never entered** | On the Harvest Moon, the lights were the family not coming out. He doesn't go where he wasn't let in. |
| **It bangs before entering a dark building** | Jill taught him to knock. |
| **Day 6: it goes for the generator** | The generator quit that night. He remembers what made the dark. |
| **Bear traps and the pegboard** | They're Jeremy's traps on Jeremy's painted outlines. "If it's not on the board, it's in the corn" is why it takes any trap left off the pegboard at nightfall. |
| **The trap race** | It comes to see whether anyone comes for you. A teammate shortens the pry: that's what should have happened. |
| **Shed lock broken from day 5** | Jeremy's lock never kept him out either. |
| **The wild corn can't be cut** | It grew overnight around the trap and has never been cut since. |
| **Moonflowers** | They first bloomed in the ring the morning after. They glow in the dark, open only at night, and turn bad if left standing at dawn. |
| **Taint (black, oily hands)** | Whatever the corn is now, it gets on you. It can follow what it has touched. |
| **The well cures Taint** | Jill's well. She scrubbed his hands at it before every supper. The pump is loud, and he always came running at the sound. |
| **Dusk: the bell rings three times** | The festival bell from 1958, rung every dusk since so the Brenn boy knows to come in. |
| **The Prize Pumpkin is gnawed** | He's hungry. |
| **The wrong count** | He wants to be counted with the farmhands, so he borrows a hat. |
| **The scarecrow moves, facing the farmhouse** | Never explained in game. Team-only: it's waiting to be called in. |
| **It rarely uses your own voice on you** | Jeremy told him to use his own voice. He's saving yours. |
| **The bank, seven days, three seasons** | The bank has leased the farm a season at a time since 1958. Paying it off (D-155) is the first time anyone has owned it since the Brenns. |
